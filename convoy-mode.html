How it works

How group travel works

Keeping several cars together on one trip, from the join code to the alerts. In the app this is called convoy mode. Updated 21/11/2026.

A normal GPS app guides one car. When three cars leave Christchurch for Queenstown together, each driver ends up following their own directions, someone gets caught at a red light, and the group spends the next hour texting "where are you?". Rōpū Mapi puts every car on one shared route and shows each driver where the others are.

Starting a group trip

  1. One person plans the route. We call them the leader. Pick the destination as usual. The route can avoid gravel roads and takes NZ state highway closures into account.
  2. The leader taps Convoy. The app makes a 6-letter code, such as PQNTMB. Share it however you like: say it out loud, text it, or post it in the group chat.
  3. Everyone else types in the code and a nickname. The nickname is just a name for the map, like "Sam" or "Blue Hilux". There's no account, password or phone number.
  4. Each car gets the leader's route. Every driver now has spoken turn-by-turn directions for the same trip.

What each driver sees

  • Every car on the map with its nickname.
  • A list of the cars with each one's speed and its gap to the leader.
  • Whether the group is together or spread out. By default a car counts as spread out beyond 250 m from the leader and as far behind beyond 500 m. You can change both distances in the settings to suit motorway or town driving.

Alerts

The app tells the group when something changes, so nobody has to watch the screen:

  • Member far behind: a car has dropped further back than the "far behind" distance.
  • Member not moving: a car has stopped, maybe for fuel or a breakdown.
  • Member wrong turn: a car has left the route. The alert clears itself once they're back on it.

Each alert type can be a pop-up with voice, a quiet banner, or turned off. A driver who finds them distracting can switch to banners only.

Stops and group decisions

Anyone in the group can look up fuel, EV charging, food and rest stops along the route, sorted by how far ahead they are. Suggest one and the rest of the group votes on it from the driving screen. That beats trying to agree on a café over speakerphone.

The leader can also remove someone from the group, for example when a car heads home early.

When the signal drops

Directions keep working without mobile coverage, because the app saves the map for your route on the phone before you leave. Live positions of the other cars need mobile data, so in a dead spot the other cars stay where they were last seen and update again when you're back in coverage. On long rural stretches it's worth agreeing on a regroup point before you set off. The tips for driving together have more on this.

Privacy on a group trip

Your position is shared only with the people in the group you joined, and only while you're in it. Our server keeps positions in memory, never in a database, and removes yours 15 seconds after you leave or disconnect. The full details are in the privacy policy.

How it compares with sharing your location

Messaging and map apps can share a live location, which tells you where someone is. It doesn't put them on your route, warn you when they stop, or let the group pick the next stop. Rōpū Mapi does all of that in the same screen you're already using for directions.

Common questions

Does Google Maps have a convoy mode?

Google Maps lets you share your live location and trip progress, but each driver still navigates on their own and there is no shared convoy view or group alerts. Rōpū Mapi is built for that: every car joins one convoy with a 6-letter code, follows the leader's route, and sees the other cars and their gaps on the map.

How many cars can join a group trip?

It is made for small groups of cars travelling together, such as a family trip or a club run. Each car joins with the same 6-letter code and its own nickname.

Does every driver need the app?

Yes. Each car that wants to appear on the map and receive directions and alerts needs Rōpū Mapi on an Android phone (Android 7.0 or later). It is free and needs no account.

Does it work without mobile signal?

Turn-by-turn directions keep working without signal because the route map is saved on the phone. Live positions of the other cars need mobile data, so they pause in dead spots and catch up when signal returns.
