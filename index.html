How it works

Three steps to travel together

A normal GPS app guides one car. When you travel with family or friends in two or three cars, someone gets stuck at the lights or misses a turn, and the phone calls start. Rōpū Mapi links the cars in a few seconds, and nobody has to sign up.

routeStep 1

Plan your route

Pick where you're going. You can avoid gravel roads, and NZ state highway closures are taken into account. The map for the route is saved on your phone before you lose signal.

shareStep 2

Share a 6-letter code

Tap Convoy in the app to get a code such as P Q N T M B. Send it in the family chat or read it out. The others type it in with a nickname and get the same route. No logins or phone numbers.

directions_carStep 3

Drive together

Everyone sees every car on the map, with its speed and how far behind it is. You get a heads-up if someone stops, falls behind or takes a wrong turn. Anyone can suggest a fuel or food stop and the group votes on it.

What is inside

For group trips, and for everyday driving

Everything here is in the current Google Play release. It uses open map data and nothing is behind a paywall.

groups Group travel

See every car, get alerts

Everyone follows the same route and sees how far apart the cars are. The group gets an alert when a car stops, drops a long way back or goes off the route. The person who started the trip can remove someone if needed.

local_gas_station Stops

Suggest a stop, vote on it

Find fuel, EV charging, food and rest stops along your route, nearest first. Anyone in the group can suggest one and the others vote from the driving screen.

record_voice_over Directions

Spoken turn-by-turn directions

Large, easy-to-read turn cards with spoken directions, and you choose how often it speaks. Shows your speed, plus the posted speed limit from open map data for reference.

construction NZ roads

Road closures and roadworks

State highway closures and roadworks come straight from NZ Transport Agency Waka Kotahi, so your route avoids known closures and warns you about roadworks ahead. You can also avoid gravel roads.

signal_cellular_off No signal

Directions keep working with no signal

The map for your route is saved on your phone, so directions keep going where mobile coverage drops out. You choose how much space it uses (100, 250 or 500 MB) and can clear it any time.

dark_mode Everyday

Night map, walking and cycling

The map turns dark at night to cut glare. Works for walking and cycling as well as driving, and remembers recent destinations.

Plain facts

Private by design, honest about its limits

Rōpū Mapi is built to be straightforward about what it collects, where its data comes from, and what it does not do.

How your privacy is protected

  • No accounts, ads, or tracking: You never sign up or hand over your email address or phone number to use the app.
  • Shared only with your group: During a group trip, your nickname and position are kept in server memory only, never in a database. They're removed 15 seconds after you leave or disconnect.
  • HTTPS everywhere: The Google Play build uses encrypted HTTPS connections only.
  • Deletable problem reports: If you submit an in-app problem report, you can remove every report sent from your phone with Delete my reports, and all reports are deleted automatically after 90 days.

Important driving & data notes

  • Posted road signs come first: Speed limits come from OpenStreetMap and open data, not the official speed limit register. Always treat them as a reference and obey posted signs.
  • No live traffic feeds: Travel times are estimates. Road closures and roadworks come from NZTA state highway data and do not cover every local street.
  • Drive at your own safe pace: Never speed or take risks to catch up with the group. Other cars' positions can lag when the signal is weak.
  • Open data powered: Built on OpenStreetMap, OpenFreeMap, OSRM, Valhalla, Overture Places, Open-Meteo, and NZTA Waka Kotahi.

Common questions

Frequently asked questions

Straight answers about travelling together, driving without signal, and privacy.

Is there a free app to keep several cars together on a road trip?

Yes. Rōpū Mapi is a free Android app made for group travel in more than one car. One person starts a group trip and shares a 6-letter code with the other cars. Everyone follows the same route with spoken directions, sees where each car is and how far behind it is, and gets an alert if someone stops or falls behind. The app calls this convoy mode.

Does everyone in the group need an account or a phone number?

No. Rōpū Mapi has no accounts, passwords or phone-number sign-ups. You type the 6-letter code and a nickname for the map. Positions are kept in server memory only while the group trip is running, and yours is removed 15 seconds after you leave.

Does Rōpū Mapi work without mobile signal?

Yes for directions. When you plan a route, Rōpū Mapi saves the map for that route on your phone, so turn-by-turn directions keep working where there is no mobile coverage. You choose how much space it uses (100, 250 or 500 MB) and can clear it at any time. Seeing the other cars live needs mobile data.

Can anyone in the group suggest a fuel or food stop?

Yes. Anyone in the group can look up fuel, EV charging, food and rest stops along the route, suggest one, and the others vote yes or no from the driving screen.

Which phones and countries is Rōpū Mapi available on?

Rōpū Mapi is available free on Google Play for Android 7.0 and up. It is currently published in New Zealand and Taiwan. There is no iOS version at this stage.
