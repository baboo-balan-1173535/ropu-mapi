Driving tips

Driving together in more than one car

Practical tips for group road trips with two or more cars (sometimes called driving in convoy), written for New Zealand roads. Updated 21/11/2026.

The one rule: every driver drives their own car at their own safe pace. Nobody speeds, runs an orange light or overtakes badly to keep up. A group that gets split for ten minutes is fine. A crash is not.

Before you leave

  • Agree on the destination and the route. Everyone should know where you're going, even without the app. If one car loses the group, the driver can still get there.
  • Pick regroup points. Choose a couple of places along the way where everyone will wait, such as a town with fuel and toilets. This matters most on long rural stretches with no signal.
  • Fill up at the start. Cars burn fuel at different rates. Starting full saves an early stop for one car while the others wait.
  • Decide who leads. The leader should know the route best and be comfortable driving at a pace the slowest vehicle can manage, such as a towing car or a campervan.
  • Set up the phones while parked. Mount each phone where the driver can see it without holding it, and get the passenger to run the app. NZ law doesn't allow drivers to hold a phone while driving.
  • Swap numbers. A phone call from a passenger is still the quickest fix when something goes wrong.

Keep a safe gap, not a tight one

It's tempting to sit close to the car in front so nobody cuts in. Don't. The NZ Road Code asks for at least a two-second gap in good conditions and four seconds when the road is wet or slippery. That applies when you're driving as a group too.

If other traffic slots in between your cars, let it. The app shows where everyone is, so you don't need to see the car ahead to know you're on the same road.

Lights, roundabouts and motorway merges

  • Traffic lights: if the lights change and split the group, the front cars carry on at normal speed. They don't stop on the road to wait. The cars behind follow their own directions and catch up.
  • Roundabouts and give-way signs: each driver judges the gap for their own car. Never follow the car ahead into a gap that was only big enough for them.
  • Motorway on-ramps: merge at your own pace. Two cars don't need to enter the motorway side by side.
  • Overtaking: overtake one vehicle at a time, only when it's safe for your car. Don't follow the car in front past a truck just because they went.

New Zealand roads have their own quirks

  • One-lane bridges: give-way rules apply to each car, not to the group. If you have to give way, the car ahead goes on without you. That's normal.
  • Gravel roads: leave more room so you're not driving into the dust and stones of the car in front. Rōpū Mapi can plan routes that avoid gravel roads.
  • No-signal stretches: parts of State Highways 6, 7, 73 and 94, and plenty of back roads, have long gaps in coverage. Directions keep working when the map for your route is saved on the phone, but live positions of the other cars pause until you're back in coverage. That's where the regroup points come in.
  • Road closures: slips and snow close state highways often. Check NZTA's road information before you leave. Rōpū Mapi routes around NZTA state highway closures it knows about, but local roads aren't covered.

When a car gets separated

  1. Don't stop on the road. If you need to wait, pull over somewhere safe and legal, well off the road.
  2. Check where they are. In Rōpū Mapi, the list of cars shows how far each one is behind the leader. A "far behind" alert usually just means lights or traffic.
  3. Head for the next regroup point. If the gap keeps growing, carry on to the agreed stop and wait there.
  4. Call if a car stops for a long time. A "not moving" alert that doesn't clear could mean a breakdown. Get a passenger to ring them. In an emergency, call 111.

Stops

Plan stops roughly every two hours, and agree on them as a group so nobody has to signal and hope. In Rōpū Mapi any member can suggest a fuel, food or rest stop along the route, and the others vote from the driving screen. When you pull in, park together if you can and leave as a group.

A quick checklist

  • Destination and route agreed
  • Regroup points chosen
  • Everyone fuelled up
  • Phones mounted, passengers running the app
  • Route map saved on each phone before leaving coverage
  • Phone numbers swapped
