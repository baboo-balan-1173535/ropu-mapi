Use cases

Group road trips with more than one car

Who travels together with Rōpū Mapi, and what it helps with. Updated 21/11/2026.

Any trip where two or more vehicles are heading to the same place has the same problem: one car gets held up and the rest don't know. Here's how travelling together in Rōpū Mapi fits different kinds of trip.

Family trips with two or three cars

Grandparents in one car, the kids split across two more, all heading to the bach for Christmas. The leader shares a code in the family chat, every car follows the same route, and nobody has to phone at each turn-off. When the kids need a toilet stop, someone proposes it and the other cars see it straight away.

Car club runs and cruise days

Club runs often mean a long line of cars on a route that only the organiser knows. With one code, every driver gets turn-by-turn directions for the organiser's route, so the group doesn't need a marshal at every intersection. The organiser can see who's dropped back and wait at the next stop.

4WD and off-the-beaten-track trips

Back-country trips often head out of mobile coverage. Save the route map on each phone before you leave town, so directions keep working when the signal goes. Live positions resume once you're back in coverage, and the "not moving" alert gives the group a heads-up if a vehicle stops for longer than expected.

Motorbike group rides

Riders can't glance at a phone the way a car passenger can. Spoken directions through a helmet headset, plus a voice alert when a rider falls behind, mean the lead rider knows when to ease off without looking back. Only use a phone on a secure mount, and set it up before you ride.

Campervan and holiday groups

Friends touring New Zealand in two or three campervans, or a rental car following a camper, travel at different speeds and stop at different times. Rōpū Mapi keeps everyone on the same route, and the group can vote on where to stop for fuel or food. NZTA closures on state highways are taken into account when planning the route.

Weddings, events and sports teams

Getting guests from the ceremony to the reception, or a sports team's cars to an away game, is a short group trip with drivers who may not know the way. Share one code and every car arrives at the same place, even if they get split up at the lights.

What you need

  • An Android phone (Android 7.0 or later) in each vehicle that wants to show on the map
  • Rōpū Mapi, free from Google Play in New Zealand and Taiwan
  • Mobile data for live positions (directions work without it once the route map is saved)
